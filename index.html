3. The Rules

1. Match the action to the person

A reflexive verb often describes something a person does to themself.

Yo me lavo. — I wash myself.

Compare: Yo lavo el carro. — I wash the car. The action is directed at the car, so no reflexive pronoun is needed.

Not every verb with -se translates as “myself” or “yourself.” Learn the whole meaning: dormirse means “to fall asleep.”

2. Choose the reflexive pronoun

The pronoun matches the subject: the person doing the action.

  • Yo → me (I → myself)
  • Tú → te (you → yourself; informal)
  • Él / ella / usted → se (he / she / formal you)
  • Nosotros / nosotras → nos (we → ourselves)
  • Vosotros / vosotras → os (informal plural you, mainly in Spain)
  • Ellos / ellas / ustedes → se (they / plural you)

3. Remove -se, then conjugate

Start with the infinitive: levantarse.

Remove -se to get levantar. Conjugate the verb for the subject and tense, then add the matching pronoun.

Yo + me + levanto = Yo me levanto. — I get up.

You can omit the subject when it is clear: Me levanto. Keep the reflexive pronoun.

4. Use the regular present-tense endings

Remove -ar, -er, or -ir to find the stem. Add the ending for the subject.

In this order: yo, tú, él/ella/usted, nosotros, vosotros, ellos/ellas/ustedes.

  • -ar: -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an
  • -er: -o, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en
  • -ir: -o, -es, -e, -imos, -ís, -en

Example: levantarse in the present tense.

  • Yo me levanto.
  • Tú te levantas.
  • Él / ella / usted se levanta.
  • Nosotros / nosotras nos levantamos.
  • Vosotros / vosotras os levantáis.
  • Ellos / ellas / ustedes se levantan.

5. Watch for stem changes

Some verbs change a vowel in their stem. In the present tense, these examples change in all forms except nosotros and vosotros.

  • Despertarse: e → ie. Me despierto. — I wake up.
  • Acostarse: o → ue. Me acuesto. — I go to bed.
  • Dormirse: o → ue. Me duermo. — I fall asleep.
  • Vestirse: e → i. Me visto. — I get dressed.

Compare: Nos despertamos. Nos acostamos. Nos dormimos. Nos vestimos.

6. Put the pronoun in the right place

Usually, put it before the conjugated verb.

Me ducho. — I take a shower.

With a conjugated verb followed by an infinitive, put the pronoun before the conjugated verb or attach it to the infinitive.

Me voy a duchar. Voy a ducharme. — Both mean “I am going to take a shower.”

With a progressive form, you also have two choices.

Me estoy duchando. Estoy duchándome. — I am taking a shower. The attached pronoun can require a written accent.

7. Negatives and commands

For a negative statement, put no before the pronoun and verb.

No me levanto temprano. — I do not get up early.

Attach the pronoun to an affirmative command. Put it before a negative command. Learn the command form too; it can differ from the present-tense form.

¡Levántate! — Get up!

¡No te levantes! — Do not get up!

8. Body parts usually take el or la

With reflexive actions, Spanish usually uses a definite article instead of “my” or “your” for body parts.

Me lavo las manos. — I wash my hands.

Me cepillo los dientes. — I brush my teeth.

The reflexive pronoun tells us whose hands or teeth are involved.
